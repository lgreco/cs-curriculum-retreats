Teaching practice
How has AI changed the way you run your classroom: lectures, labs, office hours, communication with students?
Not too much. I tried to create more AI-proof assignments and having students show more screenshots of their exploits. I created a Gemini notebookLM for students to query but it became a bit limited and I don’t think any student used it. Fewer students come to office hours but that has been a decline over the past few years – not sure if it’s attributed to only AI.
Do you have a stated AI policy for your courses? What has worked, and what hasn't?
Yes, they can use but need to cite it. Some students do cite, some don’t.
Have you used AI yourself to prepare materials, give feedback, or support students? What did you learn?
To go through one lab since the exploit python code was not working but the C was. I don’t know enough about Python to understand why. I want to auto-grade assignments, but haven’t fully implemented that process yet.
Assessment and integrity
How has AI changed the way you evaluate student work and engagement?
None for evaluation. They are all adult; they want to use an agent to run their life, kudos to them. Jokes aside, I think the very good students are still learning concepts and use AI, the F-D-C students are still not learning and are using AI. I don’t know if that’s good or not.
Which of your assessments still measure what you intend them to, and which no longer do?
I had a packet capture file that students had to tell me which three websites was visited. A few students gave me a website that didn’t exist in the file – proof that they used AI or they found something that didn’t exist. I know a lot of students are using AI for their buffer overflow assignment but AFAIK, they are still typing in the commands. I ask them to take screenshots and there is a timestamp.
Have you tried alternative formats (oral exams, in-class work, process logs, code walkthroughs, version-history review)? With what results?
No.
Content and learning outcomes
Have you changed the deliverables or emphasis of your courses in response to AI developments in your field? How?
I put a bit more emphasis on the competitions for my class. They allow AI usage but they also monitor what participants do.
What must students still be able to do without AI by the end of your course, and why?
Understand the concepts of cybersecurity such as buffer overflow. At least at a high level how it works.
What has become more important to teach because of AI (reading and evaluating code, specification, testing, debugging, judgment about tool output)?
I really don’t know. I think the concepts are still important but I can’t tell if I am being biased. Do people really need to know how encryption work anymore in 5 years? Maybe, maybe not. Based on the recent leak that a root authority used a key of 4, maybe?
Do your courses, as currently planned, prepare students for an AI-mediated workplace? What evidence do you have, from employers, alumni, or students?
I don’t know. I would like to think so. Anecdotal evidence from a few students suggest yes.
The curriculum as a whole
For the foundational sequence (COMP 141, 163, 170, 264, 271, 272, 363): where should topics be added, compressed, reordered, or retired?
I am for removing 272. It lengthens the sequence. From a biased point of view, I think 163 and 264 are still good. Just need to find a good way to teach them. 141 should go back to in person IMO.
What changes do you want to see in the foundational sequence that will make your downstream courses more productive? (This is a question I ask at every retreat.)
I think we need a streamlined sequence. E.g. students take X, Y, Z their first semester, followed by A, B, C their second semester. I know we have a sample sequence but do students actually follow that? I still see our majors take COMP 150 – why?
Also more consistency among sections. That’s something I have to explain for the CAE designation – how do I make sure different instructors teach the same content. I always hear oh XYZ taught ABC but when FGH taught ABC, that was great.
Are we consistent enough across sections and courses (AI policies, expectations, tools) that students experience a coherent program?
Probably not. See comment above
Do all students have equitable access to the AI tools our courses assume? Should the department provide or standardize tools?
Short answer: probably not. I don’t assume any access to AI tools. ChatGPT is free, CoPilot is free. It’s good to use as a clutch. We don’t have the funds to pay $20/month for 500 students. I heard we have a GPU cluster bought using lab fees – can that be used for classes?
What support would you need (time, training, tools, etc.) to make the changes you think are necessary?
I think more time to play around with the tools. Maybe more training – I think Leo has way more experience than me and I am trying to catch up but it’s hard to not distracted with the 100 other things I need to do. Maybe some easy-to-adopt tool or process from others who are more involved? With step by step instruction or demo?
